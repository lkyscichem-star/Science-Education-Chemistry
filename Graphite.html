Graphite

Giant Covalent Structure

Structure & Bonding

Carbon atoms are linked by covalent bonds. These particles are arranged in the same plane. Individual layers within the carbon sheet (graphene) are held together by weak van der Waals forces.

Delocalized Electrons

Each carbon atom is covalently bonded to 3 carbon atoms in each layer. One unbonded electron in the valence shell is present on each carbon atom. These unbonded electrons become delocalized between the sheet layers of carbon atoms. This allows movement of electrons that runs parallel to the layers.

Physical Properties

The carbon atoms form covalent bonds in each layer of the carbon sheet. Because the van der Waals forces between them are weak, layers of carbon atoms can easily slide over one another. Graphite is soft and slippery because of its unique structure.

Conductivity

Yes. The mobile electron (unbonded) can flow along the layers of hexagonally linked carbon atoms.
